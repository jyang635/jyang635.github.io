About Me

Learn more about my academic journey, research interests, and professional experience in the field of Brain-Computer Interfaces.

I'm Jun Yang, a passionate PhD candidate at the University of Technology, Sydney, where I'm exploring the fascinating world of Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) under the supervision of Prof. Sarah Johnson.

My research focuses on developing non-invasive BCI technologies that can help people with motor disabilities regain independence through assistive devices. I'm particularly interested in signal processing techniques and machine learning algorithms for decoding neural signals.

Before starting my PhD, I completed my Master's in Electrical Engineering at Tsinghua University, where I first became interested in neural engineering. In my free time, I enjoy mentoring students and sharing my passion for BCI research through public talks and workshops.

Research Focus Areas

My research interests span multiple areas within the field of Brain-Computer Interfaces, with a focus on developing practical solutions for real-world applications.

EEG Signal Processing

Developing advanced algorithms to improve the quality of EEG signals and extract meaningful information for BCI applications.

  • Noise reduction techniques
  • Feature extraction and selection
  • Signal classification algorithms

Machine Learning for BCIs

Applying machine learning techniques to decode neural signals and improve the accuracy and reliability of BCI systems.

  • Deep learning models for EEG analysis
  • Transfer learning for BCI adaptation
  • Real-time classification systems

Neurorehabilitation

Exploring how BCI technologies can be used for neurorehabilitation and improving the quality of life for individuals with disabilities.

  • Motor imagery-based rehabilitation
  • BCI-driven robotic exoskeletons
  • Cognitive training applications
