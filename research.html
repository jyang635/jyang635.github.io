Research

Explore my research interests and current projects in the field of Brain-Computer Interfaces.

Research Overview

My research focuses on developing efficient and accurate models for Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) that can interpret neural signals in real-time.

Supervisor: Prof. Arno Onken
2023 - Present
School of informatics

Research Goals

The primary goal of my research is to develop robust and user-friendly BCI systems that can translate neural signals into actionable commands, enabling individuals with severe motor disabilities to interact with their environment and communicate more effectively.

Methodology

My approach combines advanced signal processing techniques, machine learning algorithms, and user-centered design principles to create BCI systems that are both accurate and intuitive to use. I specialize in working with electroencephalogram (EEG) signals due to their non-invasive nature and practicality for real-world applications.

Impact

The outcomes of this research have the potential to significantly improve the quality of life for individuals with conditions such as spinal cord injury, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and cerebral palsy, by providing them with new means of communication and control.

Research Areas

My research spans several key areas within the field of Brain-Computer Interfaces, with a focus on practical applications and technological innovation.

EEG Signal Processing

Developing advanced algorithms to improve the quality of EEG signals and extract meaningful information for BCI applications.

  • Noise reduction and artifact removal
  • Feature extraction and selection
  • Signal enhancement for noisy environments

Machine Learning for BCIs

Applying machine learning techniques to decode neural signals and improve the accuracy and reliability of BCI systems.

  • Deep learning models for EEG analysis
  • Transfer learning for BCI adaptation
  • Real-time classification systems
